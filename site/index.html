    It keeps happening

    March 2023

    The crop that wasn't

    Simon Aarons and David Buchanan showed that Google Pixel's Markup tool wrote a cropped screenshot over the original without shortening the file, so the cut-away part could be rebuilt. Windows' Snipping Tool had the same flaw. Tracked as CVE-2023-21036 and CVE-2023-28303.

    Source

    January 2019

    Black boxes, live text

    A court filing by Paul Manafort's lawyers blacked out passages that reporters could simply select, copy and paste, revealing what the redactions were meant to hide.

    Source

    December 2012

    A photo with coordinates

    A magazine published a photo of John McAfee, then a fugitive. Its EXIF data still held the GPS position, placing him in Guatemala rather than Belize.

    Source

    October 2005

    Deleted, but tracked

    A UN report on the assassination of Rafik Hariri was circulated as a Word file. Turning on Track Changes showed names that had been deleted from the final text.

    Source

    For many files, use the command line

    The same engine ships as a single binary with no dependencies. It reads folders, writes text, JSON, SARIF or Markdown, and fails a CI job when a document about to be published leaks.

    $ go install github.com/xceg/sphinx/cmd/sphinx@latest
$ sphinx ~/Desktop/to-send/
$ sphinx reveal screenshot.png      # extract what it hides
$ sphinx seal holiday.jpg           # write holiday.sealed.jpg

    Prebuilt binaries for Linux, macOS and Windows are on the releases page. The README covers CI, pre-commit and the Go library.

    Sphinx is open source under the MIT licence. This page has no analytics, no cookies and no server: your files stay on your device.
